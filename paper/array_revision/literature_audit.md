## Defensible Novelty

Bounded lookahead, its accuracy-cost question, and its use in forests are established prior art; neither renaming it "k-sighted" nor testing k = 3 creates an algorithmic contribution. The closest forest precedent is [Donick and Lera (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8085031/); the closest recent hybrid search comparator is [Babbar et al. (2025)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/babbar25a.html).

Suggested framing, conditional on the currently reported results:

> This work is an exploratory study of how to allocate established bounded-lookahead computation across members of shallow bootstrap ensembles, rather than a new tree-induction algorithm. At a fixed final-depth limit D = 3, we compare uniformly sighted ensembles with mixtures of k = 1, 2, and 3 trees and different ensemble sizes. On the reported PMLB samples, some mixtures attain higher observed mean accuracy at comparable mean fitting time than selected lower-sight grid configurations. These observations motivate heterogeneous horizon allocation; they do not establish a general Pareto improvement, an optimal allocation policy, a diversity mechanism, or superiority over tuned random forests or modern optimal-tree solvers.

Do not claim "first heterogeneous forest," "new non-greedy induction," or "lookahead is generally not worthwhile." A bounded literature search cannot establish priority for mixing horizons. "Sparse" here means a minority of lookahead-trained members, not the leaf-count sparsity optimized by sparse-tree solvers.

## Depth Versus Horizon

Use D for the maximum final root-to-leaf number of splits, d for current node depth, and k for the number of optimized split generations including the current split. The effective horizon is h = min(k, D - d). D controls representational capacity; k controls search within that capacity. Increasing k does not increase D.

The [builder and Python scorer](/Users/mathiasvalla/Desktop/Post-doc/Papiers/Non_greedy_trees/treeple/treeple/tree/_lookahead.py:163) commit a root, then re-run the configured lookahead at the actual children; depth checks cap that search. This is receding-horizon induction, not simply fitting a depth-k tree once.

**Important consequence of D = 3:** a final tree has at most eight leaves, and k = 3 covers the entire remaining depth already at the root. Under exhaustive admissible candidates, consistent constraints/stopping, and zero additional gain threshold, the idealized additive recursion minimizes terminal training impurity over that depth-bounded class (up to the implementation's numerical tolerances). This is a mathematical/code inference, not an experimental finding. It does not certify minimum classification error, a sparsity-penalized optimum, or held-out generalization. The current benchmark therefore does not test a short horizon in substantially deeper trees; the "intermediate" characterization needs this qualification.

## Exact Complexity Issue

The [current argument, line 69](/Users/mathiasvalla/Desktop/Post-doc/Papiers/Non_greedy_trees/treeple/paper/prl_submission/main.tex:69) conflates complete-tree configuration count with optimization work.

1. A **full** depth-h binary topology has 2^h - 1 internal nodes. If each independently admits m split labels, complete assignments number m^(2^h - 1); fixing the root gives m^(2^h - 2). With data-dependent feasibility these are only idealized upper counts, not exact counts of admissible subtrees. They are not runtime lower bounds.
2. Weighted terminal impurity is additive. Writing C_0(S) = w(S) I(S), its local optimum obeys C_h(S) = min{C_0(S), min_s [C_(h-1)(S_left) + C_(h-1)(S_right)]}, subject to constraints. For each root candidate, child optima are **added**, not all combinations of left and right configurations enumerated.
3. Let E_h count candidate evaluations and assume exactly m candidates in every nonterminal recursive call, with no early stopping. Then E_0 = 0 and E_h = m + 2m E_(h-1), hence E_h = m[(2m)^h - 1]/(2m - 1). For h = 1, 2, 3 this gives m; m + 2m^2; m + 2m^2 + 4m^3, not m, m^3, m^7. With m only a uniform upper bound, the formula is an upper bound. Growth is O((2m)^h), or O(m^h) for fixed h, not the complete-assignment count.
4. This still is not wall-clock complexity: sorting, partition construction, and impurity computation cost work. For n root samples, p features, fixed class count, and m >= 1 bounding candidates in every descendant, a conservative bound for the current scorer is B sum_(q=0)^(h-1) (2m)^q, with B = O(p n log(n+1) + m n). This bounds each descendant's size by n and is deliberately loose. Here m <= p(n-1) without a candidate cap, so fixed h yields polynomial dependence on n and p. Actual fit cost also sums over committed tree nodes; ensemble cost depends on scheduling and overhead.

This factorization is visible in [Python recursion](/Users/mathiasvalla/Desktop/Post-doc/Papiers/Non_greedy_trees/treeple/treeple/tree/_lookahead.py:211) and [Cython recursion](/Users/mathiasvalla/Desktop/Post-doc/Papiers/Non_greedy_trees/treeple/treeple/tree/_lookahead_fast.pyx:231). Neither scorer shows a memoization cache across subproblems; do not imply that the reported implementation uses one. Caching and branch-and-bound are additional accelerations, not the reason the child objectives factorize. As an independent primary-source check, [MurTree Section 4.3, Proposition 3](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume23/20-520/20-520.pdf) gives O(|D| |F|^2) for basic optimal depth-two recursion on binary features, before its specialized improvements.

Suggested replacement:

> Although a full depth-k topology has m^(2^k - 1) possible split assignments under a uniform m-candidate idealization, additive leaf impurity permits independent optimization of the two children for each root candidate. Without memoization or pruning, the corresponding recursive candidate-evaluation bound is O((2m)^k), before accounting for sample-dependent sorting and partition costs and the remaining final-depth limit. The measured runtime growth is specific to this implementation and candidate set, not an unavoidable cost of every lookahead or optimal-tree method.

## Statistical and Utility Boundaries

- **Single split:** [Experimental protocol, line 84](/Users/mathiasvalla/Desktop/Post-doc/Papiers/Non_greedy_trees/treeple/paper/prl_submission/main.tex:84) supports descriptive, paired dataset-level comparisons, not split/seed stability or a general recommendation. More datasets do not replace repeated evaluation within datasets. Literature additions cannot repair this evidential limitation. Do not retrospectively describe the study as a validated model-selection procedure.
- **Budget comparison:** the 0.7 s line is a threshold on mean fitting time across the mixed-forest sample, not a per-dataset training deadline. Unless independently prespecified, call it an illustrative budget slice. Curves and a few nearby points establish observed tradeoffs, not a validated optimum or universal Pareto frontier. Keep the 67- and 57-dataset aggregates separate.
- **Utility:** U_lambda = A - lambda log(T/T_1) is an explicitly chosen preference model, not a statistical theorem or established tree-selection rule. A breakpoint Delta A / log(T/T_1) assumes T > T_1; equality makes that formula undefined, and T < T_1 reverses the inequality. Lambda has accuracy-fraction units per natural-log time ratio. No citation turns a subjective penalty into evidence that one method is generally preferable.
- **Aggregation:** log(mean T_k / mean T_1) is not mean log(T_ik / T_i1). The present utility combines macro-average accuracy with a ratio of mean times; large runtimes can dominate its cost component. Label it a sensitivity calculation and keep raw accuracy/time tradeoffs primary. The finite k = 1, 2, 3 grid does not establish any asymptotic utility claim.
- **Heterogeneity mechanism:** varying k may change member strength, shared bias, and error dependence. For averaged class scores Z_b, Var(B^(-1) sum_b Z_b) = B^(-2) sum_(b,c) Cov(Z_b, Z_c); k-mixtures alone do not determine these terms or the final argmax classification error. This identity is an algebraic observation, not a new generalization theorem. [Kuncheva and Whitaker](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1022859003006) support caution about diversity-based explanations. Replace "where it can diversify the ensemble" with "as a candidate way to allocate computation across ensemble members."
- **Model name:** with all features at every split, retain "bootstrap forest" or "bagged trees" and avoid implying a comparison against tuned conventional random forests with feature subsampling. The potential distinction is heterogeneous *optimization horizons*, not heterogeneity itself or heterogeneous final depths.

Minimum constructive revision: acknowledge direct k = 2 and forest precedents; cite the recent hybrid/scalable-search work; distinguish D from k; replace the configuration-count runtime argument; and make the novelty and conclusions explicitly conditional on the exploratory protocol. No experiments or implementation changes are proposed or performed in this audit.
