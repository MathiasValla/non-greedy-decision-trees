Bounded split optimization is an established way to address myopic induction, but its predictive value depends on capacity and the comparator. In the repeated evaluation, sparse two-sighted replacements improve a shallow, depth-matched CART ensemble at substantial additional fitting cost. The mean advantage shrinks with deeper members, uniform two-sighted induction can reduce unrestricted-depth accuracy, and training-selected conventional forests achieve higher benchmark-average accuracy. These findings support a conditional benefit for constrained shallow ensembles, not general mixed-forest superiority or a validated fixed-time policy. Future work should compare training-selected, resource-bounded mixtures with modern hybrid search while accounting for selection cost and testing larger, independent tasks.
