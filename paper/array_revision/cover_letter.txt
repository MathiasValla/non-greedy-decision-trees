Please find the revised manuscript ARRAY-D-26-03529, previously titled
"Sparse farther-sighted trees can improve forest accuracy-time tradeoffs" and
now titled "A few farther-sighted trees can improve shallow forests".

The article has been substantially improved in both evidence and presentation.
Its clearer storyline asks whether a few farther-sighted trees can improve a
shallow forest without requiring every member to search farther. Sparse
replacements improve mean accuracy under both feature policies studied. In
Figure 2, a 20-member forest with 15 CART and five two-sighted members reaches
74.06% mean accuracy, compared with 73.02% for 200 greedy members, with about
29% less benchmark-mean member-fitting work. Training-only tuning of both
conventional and expanded families also gives the inclusive family higher
benchmark-mean accuracy (80.56% versus 78.99%). The revised article develops
these positive findings more directly, reduces execution detail, and brings
the main limitations together in Section 6.1.

Reviewer 1's methodological concerns have been addressed through five paired
stratified outer partitions on all 57 datasets, deeper and unrestricted tree
settings, matched-depth unpruned CART and farther-sighted controls, training-only
selection for both conventional and mixed-capable families, and paired
statistical analysis with one eleven-contrast Holm adjustment. Three-sighted
members are included in the completed fixed and tuned evaluations. The study
also corrects bootstrap-slot and timing issues, removes the ad hoc utility,
and treats bounded split search as established prior work.

The accompanying response retains the detailed numerical results, validation
scope, and methodological safeguards. The Figure 2 example compares observed
benchmark means, not exact equal-time fits or a validated per-dataset budget;
its fitting work excludes preparation, prediction, and selection. The tuned
inclusive mean gain is 1.57 percentage points [0.30, 3.28], alongside a zero
median and a Holm-adjusted signed-rank p-value of 0.48838. Heterogeneous effects,
benchmark sensitivities, and much higher selection and refit costs qualify the
interpretation. The expanded family can select pure forests as well as
mixtures, so its entire gain is not attributed to sparse mixing.
